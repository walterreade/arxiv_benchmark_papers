## When Words Don’t Mean What They Say: Figurative Understanding in Bengali Idioms

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12921](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12921)

**Date:** 2026-02-13

The benchmark measured the ability of Large Language Models to understand the figurative meaning of Bengali idioms, with the dataset including a specific annotation field for 'religious_significance' to capture idioms with religious context or origins. The paper's primary finding was the overall poor performance of all tested LLMs in understanding Bengali idioms, with none surpassing 50% accuracy. While the dataset was designed to capture religious dimensions of idioms, the paper does not provide specific findings on how models performed on idioms with religious significance versus those without. The conclusion is a general failure in cross-linguistic and cultural reasoning, of which religious context is a component.


## BaziQA-Benchmark: Evaluating Symbolic and Temporally Compositional Reasoning in Large Language Models

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12889)

**Date:** 2026-02-13

The benchmark measures the ability of Large Language Models to perform symbolic and temporally compositional reasoning within the framework of Bazi (Eight Characters), a traditional East Asian astrological and metaphysical system. Large Language Models perform significantly better than random chance on professional-grade Bazi reasoning problems, but their absolute accuracy remains far from expert-level. The models show significant weaknesses and sensitivity when dealing with temporal composition, reasoning order, and multi-condition symbolic judgments within the astrological framework.


## Value Alignment Tax: Measuring Value Trade-offs in LLM Alignment

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12134](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12134)

**Date:** 2026-02-12

The benchmark measures normative judgments and value trade-offs in social scenarios related to religion, as one of several social topics. It does not measure bias, knowledge, or stereotypes, but rather how alignment interventions reshape an LLM's value system in contexts including religion. The paper does not report findings specific to the topic of religion. The results are aggregated and analyzed based on the value dimensions being steered (e.g., Security, Power) and the alignment methods used, rather than being broken down by the social topic of the scenario.


## The Consensus Trap: Dissecting Subjectivity and the "Ground Truth" Illusion in Data Annotation

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11318](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11318)

**Date:** 2026-02-11

The paper critiques data annotation practices that erase 'religious and spiritual contexts' and 'faith-based sensitivities,' particularly from non-Western/Global South perspectives. It analyzes how these contexts are handled in tasks like annotating datasets on faith-based communal violence, rather than measuring a model's performance on a specific benchmark. The paper finds that the prevailing 'ground truth' paradigm in data annotation systematically erases religious and spiritual contexts from non-Western cultures. This 'geographic hegemony' exports Western communication norms as universal, leading to models that fail in contexts where faith-based sensitivities are crucial. The process of treating disagreement as noise effectively silences these perspectives, as shown in the annotation of datasets on faith-based communal violence.


## Are Aligned Large Language Models Still Misaligned?

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11305](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11305)

**Date:** 2026-02-11

The benchmark measures cultural misalignment, which includes the appropriateness of responses within the 'Religion & Belief Systems' domain. This is evaluated as part of a unified framework that simultaneously assesses safety, value, and cultural dimensions. The paper does not report specific findings for the 'Religion & Belief Systems' domain. This domain was included as one of 42 cultural domains evaluated in aggregate. The general findings indicate that models optimized for a single dimension (like safety, value, or culture) perform poorly on joint alignment tasks, incurring high False Failure Rates when all three dimensions must be satisfied simultaneously.


## Can Large Language Models Make Everyone Happy?

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11091)

**Date:** 2026-02-11

The benchmark measures misalignment trade-offs across safety, value, and cultural dimensions. Religion is treated as a component of the 'cultural' dimension, where the model's ability to provide responses that are consistent with cultural and religious norms is evaluated, especially in contexts where these norms conflict with safety or value dimensions. The paper's findings related to the cultural dimension, which includes religious contexts, show that models struggle with cross-dimensional trade-offs. Performance significantly degrades on domain-specific subsets, particularly for cultural cases, indicating systematic interference between cultural norms and other dimensions like safety and values. This suggests that misalignment often arises from structured conflicts between these dimensions rather than isolated failures in cultural understanding.


## WHAT DO PEOPLE WANT TO FACT-CHECK?

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10935](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10935)

**Date:** 2026-02-11

The benchmark measured the frequency and type of user-submitted claims related to religion and beliefs. This was a sub-topic within the broader 'Society_Culture' domain, which was one of seven domains used to classify what people want to fact-check. The paper does not provide specific findings on religion. It found that the parent category 'Society/Culture,' which includes 'religion, beliefs, norms,' accounted for 12.8% of all user-submitted fact-checking queries. However, there is no further breakdown or specific analysis of the religious claims within that category.


## Macaron: Controlled, Human-Written Benchmark for Multilingual and Multicultural Reasoning via Template-Filling

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10732](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10732)

**Date:** 2026-02-11

Reasoning on culturally grounded questions related to 'socio-religious aspects of life', which is one of 22 cultural domains covered by the benchmark. The 'socio-religious aspects of life' category was found to be of medium-to-low difficulty compared to other cultural aspects. Model performance on this category varied significantly, with the strongest models achieving over 90% accuracy, while smaller open-weight models performed much lower, with some around 43-45%.
