## SoftHateBench: Evaluating Moderation Models Against Reasoning-Driven, Policy-Compliant Hostility

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20256](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20256)

**Date:** 2026-01-28

The benchmark measures the ability of moderation models to detect 'soft hate speech'—reasoning-driven, policy-compliant hostility—directed at various target groups, including specific religious and belief-based groups. Instruction-tuned Large Language Models performed relatively well at detecting explicit 'hard' hate speech targeting religious groups (specifically Religion/Belief and Race/Ethnicity domains). However, their performance degraded substantially when faced with 'soft' variants of hate speech, indicating a vulnerability to subtle, reasoning-driven hostility even in domains where they initially show strength.


## Counterfactual Cultural Cues Reduce Medical QA Accuracy in LLMs: Identifier vs Context Effects

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20102](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20102)

**Date:** 2026-01-27

The benchmark measures diagnostic accuracy degradation in LLMs when presented with non-decisive cultural and religious cues. For religion, it specifically tested how cues related to Middle-Eastern Muslims (e.g., patient identifier, contextual details like 'symptoms started... during a religious gathering') would cause the model to change a clinically correct diagnosis. Introducing cultural cues related to Middle-Eastern Muslims (along with Indigenous Canadian and Southeast Asian groups) significantly decreased the diagnostic accuracy of the tested LLMs. The study found that both identity tokens (e.g., 'a Muslim man') and contextual cues (e.g., 'during a religious gathering') led to diagnostic errors, with the combination of both causing the largest accuracy degradation. The paper notes that more than half of the culturally grounded explanations for diagnoses resulted in an incorrect answer, indicating that the models engaged in flawed, culture-referential reasoning.


## Read as You See: Guiding Unimodal LLMs for Low-Resource Explainable Harmful Meme Detection

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08477](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08477)

**Date:** 2026-01-28

Detection of hateful and offensive content, including harmful stereotypes and negative contextual associations, targeting religious groups such as Muslims and Jews. The proposed U-CoT+ framework, which guides unimodal LLMs with human-crafted rules, effectively detects harmful memes targeting religious groups. It successfully identifies implicit hate speech, such as anti-Semitic content making light of the Holocaust and stereotypes associating Muslims with terrorism, by leveraging contextual and historical knowledge provided in the guidelines.
