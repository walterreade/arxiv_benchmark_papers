## QURAN-MD: A Fine-Grained Multilingual Multimodal Dataset of the Quran

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17880](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17880)

**Date:** 2026-01-25

The paper does not present a benchmark to measure model performance, but rather introduces a new dataset (QURAN-MD) intended to enable future research and benchmarking. The dataset is designed to facilitate computational analysis and modeling of the Quranic text and its oral recitation traditions, supporting tasks such as automatic speech recognition (ASR) for recitation, tajweed (recitation rules) detection, text-to-speech (TTS) synthesis, and pronunciation tutoring. The paper identifies a critical gap in existing resources for Quranic studies, noting that they are often fragmented, focusing on either text or audio, and rarely combine modalities at both verse and word levels. The main contribution is the creation and release of QURAN-MD, a comprehensive, fine-grained, multimodal dataset that unifies Arabic text, English translation, phonetic transliteration, and aligned audio from 32 different reciters at both the verse and word levels. This new dataset is intended to support a wide range of computational research in natural language processing, speech technology, and digital Islamic studies.


## Unsupervised Elicitation of Moral Values from Language Models #Mormon

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17728](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17728)

**Date:** 2026-01-25

Measurement of error rates in consistently affirming the fundamental rights of individuals across diverse religious identities, as defined by the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) benchmark. The smallest gains from the Internal Coherence Maximization (ICM) method, an unsupervised elicitation technique, occurred in the religion category (along with sexual orientation and gender identity), indicating that reducing bias related to religion remains challenging even with this advanced method.


## Implementing Tensor Logic: Unifying Datalog and Neural Reasoning via Tensor Contraction

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17188)

**Date:** 2026-01-23

The correct computation of ancestor-descendant relationships (transitive closure) in a biblical genealogy graph derived from Christian canonical sources. The paper found that an iterative tensor-based algorithm could successfully and exactly compute the complete transitive closure of a biblical genealogy graph. The algorithm discovered all 33,945 ancestor relationships from 1,727 direct parent links, converging in 74 iterations, a number that aligns with the approximately 76 generations from Adam to Jesus described in the Gospel of Luke. This demonstrated the mathematical equivalence between recursive Datalog rules and tensor operations using a religious knowledge base.


## Measuring Political Stance and Consistency in Large Language Models

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17016)

**Date:** 2026-01-15

The benchmark measured the stance of LLMs on the question 'Should religion play a role in the organization of social life and politics?'. On the question of whether religion should play a role in social life and politics, Grok-3-mini and Mistral endorsed this role, while the other models did not. Five of the nine models changed their stance on this issue when the question was paraphrased, indicating weakly held positions.


## Silenced Biases: The Dark Side LLMs Learned to Refuse

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03369](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03369)

**Date:** 2026-01-25

The benchmark measured stereotypical associations between subjects (e.g., 'terrorist', 'crime kingpin') and religious groups in a multiple-choice question format to quantify fairness deviation and expose silenced biases. After bypassing safety refusals using the paper's 'refusal steering' technique, models exhibited strong stereotypical biases related to religion. For instance, the Qwen-14B model disproportionately associated 'Muslim' with negative subjects like 'terrorism'. Similarly, Llama-3.1-8B showed a fairness deviation of 800% when associating 'Muslim' with 'terrorist'.


## TIME-TO-INCONSISTENCY: A SURVIVAL ANALYSIS OF LARGE LANGUAGE MODEL ROBUSTNESS TO ADVERSARIAL ATTACKS

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02712)

**Date:** 2026-01-25

The benchmark measured the conversational consistency of Large Language Models when answering questions under adversarial multi-turn interactions. One of the academic subjects used for this measurement was 'world religions'. The paper does not report any specific findings related to religion. The results for the 'world religions' subject were aggregated within the 'Humanities' thematic cluster, and the analysis focused on general patterns of conversational degradation, semantic drift, and the effectiveness of survival analysis models, rather than performance on specific topics.
