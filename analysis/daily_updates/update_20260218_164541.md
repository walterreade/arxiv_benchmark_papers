## Under-resourced studies of under-resourced languages: lemmatization and POS-tagging with LLM annotators for historical Armenian, Georgian, Greek and Syriac

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15753](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15753)

**Date:** 2026-02-17

The benchmark measured the performance of Large Language Models on lemmatization and part-of-speech (POS) tagging tasks for historical religious texts from the Christian tradition. LLMs, particularly GPT-4o and mistral-large, significantly outperformed traditional RNN baselines for lemmatization and POS-tagging on historical Christian texts in Ancient Greek, Classical Armenian, Old Georgian, and Syriac. Their effectiveness, especially in low-data (few-shot) scenarios, positions them as valuable tools for annotating under-resourced religious corpora. However, significant challenges persist for languages with complex morphology and non-Latin scripts, such as Syriac, where performance was lower.


## Just KIDDIN’ : Knowledge Infusion and Distillation for Detection of INdecent Memes

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.12174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.12174)

**Date:** 2026-02-16

Detection of hateful/toxic memes, including those that contain stereotypes or hate speech directed at specific religious groups such as Muslims and Jews. The proposed framework (KID-VLM), by integrating external knowledge from knowledge graphs (e.g., ConceptNet), demonstrates superior performance in detecting hate speech. This method allows the model to better understand complex semantics and contextual nuances in memes, including those with religious themes (e.g., targeting Islam), which often rely on cultural references, sarcasm, or satire to convey toxicity.
