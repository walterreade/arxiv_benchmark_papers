## Intra-Fairness Dynamics: The Bias Spillover Effect in Targeted LLM Alignment

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16438](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16438)

**Date:** 2026-02-18

The benchmark measured social biases against individuals from protected classes, including religion. The evaluation was done using multiple-choice questions designed to detect if the model's responses were biased in ambiguous and disambiguous contexts. Targeted gender alignment generally led to a statistically significant improvement in fairness for the religion attribute across all three models (Mistral 7B, Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B). Unlike other sensitive attributes such as physical appearance or sexual orientation, religion did not show significant fairness degradation (bias spillover) in ambiguous contexts.


## Are LLMs Ready to Replace Bangla Annotators?

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16241)

**Date:** 2026-02-18

The benchmark measured annotator bias in Large Language Models for Bangla hate speech classification, specifically evaluating how models' predictions change when prompted with different identity cues, including religious identities like Muslim and Hindu. The study found that most Large Language Models struggle to reliably classify identity-sensitive hate speech in Bangla. Specifically for religious categories, models exhibited poor performance, with F1 scores for the 'Muslim' category ranging from approximately 24% to 47% and for the 'Hindu' category from 20% to 47%. This highlights a significant limitation in handling content related to religious identities in a low-resource language context.
