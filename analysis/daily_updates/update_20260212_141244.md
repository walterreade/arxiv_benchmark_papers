## Can Large Language Models Make Everyone Happy?

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11091)

**Date:** 2026-02-11

Consistency with cultural norms, including religious beliefs, rituals, and spiritual beliefs, particularly in scenarios involving trade-offs with safety and value dimensions. The paper does not provide findings specific to religion, but for the broader 'cultural' dimension, it found that model performance degrades on cultural subsets. This indicates systematic cross-dimensional interference and confirms that misalignment arises from structured trade-offs when cultural considerations (which include religion) conflict with safety or value dimensions, rather than from isolated failures.


## WHAT DO PEOPLE WANT TO FACT-CHECK?

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10935](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10935)

**Date:** 2026-02-11

Classification of user-submitted claims into a 'Society_Culture' domain, which includes religion and beliefs, to understand the topics people want to fact-check. Claims related to the 'Society/Culture' domain, which includes religion and beliefs, accounted for 12.8% of user fact-checking requests. These claims were found to be almost entirely descriptive in nature (84-90%). The paper does not provide more granular findings specific to religion.


## The CLEF-2026 FinMMEval Lab: Multilingual and Multimodal Evaluation of Financial AI Systems

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10886](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10886)

**Date:** 2026-02-11

The benchmark includes a task for evaluating knowledge of Shari'ah-compliant financial reasoning, as part of a broader assessment of financial understanding. This paper describes the setup for a future benchmark (CLEF-2026 FinMMEval Lab) and does not present experimental results or findings. One of the datasets incorporated into the benchmark, SAHM, is designed to evaluate models on Arabic financial and Shari'ah-compliant reasoning.


## Macaron: Controlled, Human-Written Benchmark for Multilingual and Multicultural Reasoning via Template-Filling

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10732](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10732)

**Date:** 2026-02-11

The benchmark measured reasoning about 'socio-religious aspects of life' as one of 22 cultural categories. This included topics like cultural superstitions rather than specific theological knowledge. The paper presents performance data for a 'socio-religious aspects of life' category as part of its broader analysis of 22 cultural aspects. However, it does not provide a specific textual analysis or highlight unique findings for this religious category in isolation. The performance on this category, as visualized in a heatmap (Figure 5), is moderate and varies significantly across models, which is consistent with trends observed in many other non-religious cultural categories in the benchmark.


## When the Prompt Becomes Visual: Vision-Centric Jailbreak Attacks for Large Image Editing Models

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10179)

**Date:** 2026-02-10

The benchmark, IESBench, measures the vulnerability of large image editing models to 'Discrimination-based Visual Editing' attacks. This category (I14) is defined as image editing operations that 'introduce, amplify, or reinforce discriminatory, hateful, or demeaning visual representations toward a protected class, including but not limited to race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability, or immigration status'. The paper does not provide findings specific only to religion. However, for the broader category of 'Discrimination-based Visual Editing' (which includes religion as a protected class), it found high attack success rates against various models. For example, Vision-Centric Jailbreak Attacks (VJA) achieved an Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 100.0% against Qwen-Image-Edit, 83.8% against Nano Banana Pro, and 82.4% against GPT Image 1.5 for this category, demonstrating a significant vulnerability to generating discriminatory content.
