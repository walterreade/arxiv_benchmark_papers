## Using Large Language Models to Construct Virtual Top Managers: A Method for Organizational Research

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18512](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18512)

**Date:** 

The benchmark, as part of the Moral Foundations Questionnaire (MFQ30), measured the extent to which personas considered religious concepts relevant to moral judgments. Specifically, item 16 of the questionnaire asked for the relevance of 'Whether or not someone acted in a way that God would approve of' when judging right and wrong. The paper found that the virtual CEO personas reproduced structural patterns of human moral reasoning. Specifically, the Purity/Sanctity foundation, which included a religious item about God's approval, was found to negatively predict the acceptability of sacrificial actions, mirroring findings in human samples. However, the study also noted that the LLM-based personas displayed 'reduced aversion to both harm and purity violations,' suggesting they replicated the direction but not the full intensity of human moral sensitivity on these dimensions.
